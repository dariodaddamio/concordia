# UI preferences

Harness owns these general interaction and presentation preferences. Each project
owns its design system: tokens, typography, palette, components, recipes and
product rules. Apply these preferences through that project's design system.

## Purpose and information

- Design behavior before pages: identify applicable hover, focus, selected,
  disabled, loading, empty, success and error states.
- Keep task, state, consequence, labels, units and controls adjacent.
- Use spacing, alignment and type before adding containers. Avoid nested cards
  and decorative frames that add no information or affordance.
- Graphics, status and animation must communicate real information, an action
  or identity. Do not invent telemetry or technical captions.

## Interaction and accessibility

- Preserve accessible names, keyboard operation, visible focus and non-color
  cues. Keep required information available without hover or tooltips.
- Support narrow viewports, touch targets and reduced motion. Preserve native
  scrolling and meaningful pointer behavior.
- Keep hover, focus and selection geometry stable. Reserve space for feedback
  and loading content; do not shift controls or swap transient labels.
- Use semantic project roles for color, spacing, type and motion. Verify both
  supported themes; do not copy another project's visual identity.

## Forms and overlays

- Give controls persistent labels, preserve typed values and explain invalid
  input. Prevent stale asynchronous results from replacing newer input.
- Keep draft input separate from committed values where editing requires it.
- Give overlays clear dismissal behavior, keyboard access and correct focus
  restoration. Protect unfinished work under the project's confirmation rules.
- Communicate progress, errors and completion through accessible feedback.
  Motion must have a useful purpose and a reduced-motion equivalent.

## Review

- Reuse the project's tokens, recipes and primitives before adding new ones.
- Review actual keyboard, pointer and touch behavior alongside appearance.
  Verify applicable states, responsive layouts, scrolling and focus.
- Put component APIs, exact dimensions, style recipes and product-specific
  exceptions in the owning project's design system.

## Inspiration sources

- Research the interaction or information problem. Record useful patterns and
  synthesize them through the project's identity and existing components.
- Use relevant design references as evidence. The project owns its visual
  reference collection; references do not become a second design system.
